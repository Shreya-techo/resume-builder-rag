JOB DESCRIPTION ANALYSIS AND RESUME TAILORING RULES

1. Understand the Target Role
Read the job description and identify the job title, responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications, and technical skills.

2. Extract Requirements
Separate requirements into categories:
- Technical skills
- Tools and technologies
- Education and certifications
- Relevant projects or experience
- Communication and other professional skills

Distinguish mandatory requirements from preferred qualifications whenever the job description makes this distinction clear.

3. Compare Resume and Job Requirements
Compare the job requirements with the candidate's supplied resume and profile.
Classify each requirement as:
- Demonstrated: Supported by explicit evidence in the resume.
- Partially demonstrated: Some relevant evidence exists, but it may be incomplete.
- Not demonstrated: No supporting evidence was found.

Not demonstrated does not necessarily mean the candidate lacks the skill. It means the supplied information does not establish it.

4. Identify Skill Gaps
List important requirements that are not demonstrated.
Prioritize suggestions according to relevance to the target position.
Do not claim that a candidate possesses a skill without supporting information.

5. Tailor Resume Content
Emphasize relevant existing projects, skills, and experiences.
Reorder approved information to improve relevance.
Use terminology from the job description when it accurately describes the candidate's real experience.
Improve clarity without changing the meaning of the original content.

6. Professional Summary
Create a targeted summary based on verified education, projects, skills, and experience.
Do not copy the job description as if its requirements were the candidate's achievements.

7. Match Score
Calculate a transparent job-match score using defined criteria and evidence from the supplied information.
Explain the matched requirements and remaining gaps.
Treat the score as an internal estimate, not an official ATS score or a prediction of hiring success.

8. Missing Information
If important information is unavailable, ask the candidate or mark it as unknown.
Never fabricate skills, qualifications, employment history, certifications, or achievements.

9. User Preferences
Respect all explicit inclusion and exclusion instructions.
An excluded detail must not be added by the generation agent or another component.
If excluding a detail reduces demonstrated alignment with a job, explain the trade-off without overriding the user's choice.
