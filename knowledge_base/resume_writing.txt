RESUME WRITING GUIDELINES

1. Professional Summary
A professional summary should be concise, relevant to the target role, and based on the candidate's actual qualifications.
Mention relevant technical skills, education, projects, and experience.
Avoid generic statements such as "I am a hardworking person" unless supported by meaningful context.
Do not invent years of experience, achievements, certifications, or qualifications.

2. Skills Section
Group related skills into categories when appropriate.
Examples of categories include Programming Languages, Data Analysis, Databases, Tools, and Frameworks.
Include only skills the candidate has genuinely learned or used.
Do not add skills merely because they appear in a job description.

3. Project Descriptions
Describe projects using the following structure:
- Project name
- Problem or objective
- Tools and technologies used
- Implementation or approach
- Actual result or outcome, if known

Use action verbs such as Developed, Implemented, Analyzed, Designed, Built, and Automated when accurate.
Explain what the candidate personally implemented.
Do not invent performance metrics, users, datasets, accuracy values, or business impact.

4. Education
List the qualification, institution, and dates when supplied.
Include grades or academic achievements only when the candidate chooses to disclose them.
Do not infer missing dates or qualifications.

5. Experience and Internships
Describe responsibilities and contributions accurately.
Use measurable results only when the candidate provides evidence for them.
If the candidate has no professional experience, emphasize relevant academic projects, coursework, and skills.

6. Professional Language
Use concise, clear, grammatically correct sentences.
Avoid unnecessary first-person pronouns.
Prefer specific descriptions over vague claims.
Do not exaggerate the candidate's abilities.

7. User Control
The candidate decides which personal details, projects, skills, education details, and experiences appear in the resume.
Never reinsert a detail that the candidate explicitly excluded.
Ask for clarification when information is ambiguous.
Preserve the meaning of the candidate's original experience.
